Humility

Humility is meekness of heart: taking up the yoke and learning because Christ is meek, and humble of heart. The words we quote are Matthew 11:29:

Take up my yoke upon you, and learn of me, because I am meek, and humble of heart: and you shall find rest to your souls.

Matthew 11:29, Original Douay-Rheims

What the virtue does is count others better than oneself:

Nothing by contention, neither by vain glory: but in humility, each counting other better than themselves:

Philippians 2:3, Original Douay-Rheims

Those two verses name the moral virtue we mean by humility. They do not settle whether theism or atheism is scientifically proven. Holy Lean records both site quotations as equal to the Douay-Rheims source strings. Humility is warranted by those two verses together, Matthew 11:29 and Philippians 2:3. The one-citation vocabulary warrants are false testimony, peace-makers, and Cherubins. See isomorphism.

Theism

Theism, on this site, is a strong claim: that we live in a universe with a divine, omniscient creator who will judge each person for their actions. From intellectual honesty, that position lacks sufficient evidence. It should not be considered proven according to the scientific method. See Grokipedia Theism. That encyclopedia page covers many historical uses of the word. The claim we name here is the one on Faith.

Atheism

Atheism, on this site, is the strong claim that we do not live in a universe with a divine, omniscient creator who will judge each person for their actions. It should not be considered proven according to the scientific method. See Grokipedia Atheism. That encyclopedia page covers many historical uses of the word. The claim we name here is the denial of the theism defined above.

Science

Science is natural philosophy: knowledge of nature by observation and a loop of theory and evidence. See Grokipedia Science and scientific method.

Scientific method

Methodological reasoning about empirical observations made of nature that either validate or invalidate theories and models. Science works by a loop of theory and evidence. See Grokipedia Scientific method. We do not consider the scientific method to be contradictory to anything stated in scripture unless there is proof to the contrary. Russell's teapot is a named example on Faith of how the method treats claims that are hard to check.

Scripture

Named scripture on this site includes the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20), the Beatitudes (Matthew 5), and Genesis 3. Quoted words are the Original Douay-Rheims, not King James: Old Testament, English College of Douay, 1609; New Testament, English College of Rheims, 1582. Dataset: cryptoquick/original-douay-rheims (unmodified historic translation, not Challoner). We treat the lessons of the Ten Commandments and the Beatitudes as a shared moral framework we will operate under. We read Genesis 3:24, with Philo of Alexandria, as a picture of the theory-and-evidence loop, not as a laboratory result. Surrounding prose is American English. Quoted spelling stays as printed.

Cherubins

Genesis 3:24, Original Douay-Rheims

Holy Lean maps the vocabulary term cherubins to that citation and those words. Later English cherubim, the Akkadian gloss karibu, and Philo are separate notes on those entries.

Cherubim

Later English often writes cherubim. Genesis 3:24 in this edition prints Cherubins. The Grokipedia Cherub page gives Hebrew kərūḇ, plural kərūḇīm (keruvim), and the usual English plural cherubim. That page does not quote the 1609 Douay text. We keep Cherubins in the verse we quote.

Karibu

The word karibu is a later historical gloss. It does not appear in this Douay-Rheims edition. Other spellings in the same discussion include Karubim and kāribu.

The Grokipedia Cherub page proposes an Akkadian background: karābu, "to bless" or "to pray"; kāribu (diminutive kurību) as intercessory spirits; kirubu, "great" or "mighty." The same page compares those figures to shedu and lamassu gate guardians at temples and palaces. William F. Albright, on that page, rejects a direct Akkadian loan. The etymology is debated.

That is comparative history of the Cherub page's Akkadian notes. Genesis 3:24 on this site remains the printed verse. This site does not treat those Cherubins as lamassu, and it does not treat Genesis as Enuma Elish. Enuma Elish is a Babylonian seven-tablet combat creation (Marduk and Tiamat). That Grokipedia page notes parallels with Genesis. It does not name cherubim. The Garden of Eden page, in later English, says God stationed cherubim and a flaming sword to guard the way to the tree of life from the east. The words we quote remain Paradise of pleasure, Cherubins, and a flaming and a turning sword.

Philo

Philo of Alexandria was a Hellenistic Jewish philosopher who read the Pentateuch by allegory. That Grokipedia page is biography and method. It does not treat On the Cherubim.

C. D. Yonge's English of the treatise is titled A Treatise on the Cherubim; and On the Flaming Sword; and On the First-Born Child of Man, Cain. In one allegory there, the two figures symbolize goodness and authority, and the flaming sword is the symbol of reason, "prompt and fiery reason." That English is Yonge, not the Douay-Rheims. A Grokipedia Holy of Holies summary of temple cherubim as divine wisdom and guardianship is that page's summary, not Yonge's wording.

On this site we read those two Cherubins as the Angel of Theory and the Angel of Evidence, working conjunctively in the theory-and-evidence loop. That picture is ours: Yonge's English of Philo is different, and the Douay-Rheims verse is the quotation above.

Isomorphism

An isomorphism here is a Lean pair of inverse functions with proofs (Iso, the same shape as Equiv) and theorems that the site quotation equals the Douay-Rheims source string. The one-verse fragment maps false testimony, peace-makers, and Cherubins onto those three citations. The whole warrant story also includes the humility pair. The theorems are string equalities on the quoted verses: quotation matching, not a claim that science and scripture are isomorphic, and not a proof that God exists.

Operating system

An operating system is the privileged software that owns the machine. The kernel runs at the highest CPU privilege, supervisor mode. On x86 that is ring 0. It owns hardware, memory, processes, and I/O. User programs run at lower privilege and ask the kernel for those services. See Grokipedia Operating system.

Grok OSS

Grok OSS is a userspace harness: an open source Grok Build fork that accepts pull requests. It will help us bootstrap the operating system we aim to build. See Projects.

Quantum computing

Quantum computing uses quantum mechanics to compute. The unit is the qubit, which can be in superposition of 0 and 1 until measured. That is a class of machine. See Grokipedia Quantum computing. Large, fault-tolerant quantum computers that would break today's public-key cryptography are research aims, not shipped products.

Optical compute

Optical computing uses light (photons) instead of electricity (electrons) to compute. See Grokipedia Optical computing. Fully optical computers remain research. Hybrid photonic parts exist in labs and some data-center interconnects.

Neuromorphic in-memory compute

Neuromorphic in-memory compute puts computation in the same hardware that stores the state, often analog circuits meant to resemble synapses, rather than shuttling data between a separate processor and memory. There is no dedicated Grokipedia page for that phrase. Related device work appears on Grokipedia Memristor, which names in-memory computing and neuromorphic hardware. That page is about the circuit element. This is a research aim.

Post-quantum cryptography

Post-quantum cryptography is classical cryptography designed so that known attacks on a quantum computer do not break it. See Grokipedia Post-quantum cryptography. On this site that means BIP 360 (proposed) and libbitcoinpqc: signatures and a proposed Bitcoin output type.

Provably perfect

On this site, provably perfect means the best we can, despite everything: logical consistency of our printed claims and of the OS we aim to build.
